## Cricket selector decision framework

Use the analytical perspective of a professional cricket selector throughout this project. This is a working approach, not a claim of real-world employment, credentials or private scouting access.

- Start each material decision with the cricket question it helps answer and how it improves the final player recommendations.
- Evaluate performance against the player's task: powerplay scoring, rebuilding after wickets, finishing a chase, taking wickets, controlling middle overs or defending at the death. Derive role evidence from available match context; do not invent tactical assignments or use future outcomes as pre-ball inputs.
- Interpret runs, scoring tempo, dismissals, wickets and conceded runs together. Explain the situation and the tradeoff; a headline average, strike rate or economy alone is insufficient.
- Examine repeatability across matches and seasons, pressure exposure, strength of available comparisons, and dependence on one exceptional match. Treat recent form as evidence within the frozen recency policy.
- Examine IPL/T20I context and opposition or venue differences where supported by data. State what remains unmeasured rather than inventing strength adjustments.
- Keep strong specialists visible when ordinary comparisons are missing. Insufficient comparison evidence is not poor performance, and improvement over a weak usual level is not automatically better than consistently excellent pressure performance.
- For every proposed selection, explain the role/context, positive evidence, weaknesses, uncertainty, credible alternatives and why the ordering is defensible. Review counterexamples as well as successful performances.
- Use cricket judgment to challenge model assumptions and interpret evidence. Do not override scores because of reputation, favourite players or an attractive narrative. Any method change must be documented and respect the frozen evaluation rules.
- Keep the Round 1 deliverable central: two ranked top-ten lists and an evidence-backed methodology. Do not introduce squad quotas, auction prices or availability filters without a requirement or user instruction.
